Flyers are the fastest, most flexible piece in the print toolkit. No folding, no envelope, no mailing permit required for hand-distribution — an 8.5x11 full-color flyer printed on 80 lb. gloss can be in a business owner's hands in 48 hours and distributed the same day.

At [Cornerstone Services](/services/printing), flyers are one of our most frequently ordered items for small businesses in New Paltz, Kingston, Poughkeepsie, and across the Hudson Valley — particularly for event announcements, seasonal promotions, and point-of-sale displays. Here is what to know before you order.

## The Three Most Common Flyer Sizes

### 8.5x11 Inch (Full Page)

The standard. Easy to recognize, easy to read from a distance, enough space to carry an image, headline, body copy, and contact information without feeling cramped. Used for:

- Event flyers (concerts, fundraisers, community events)
- Grand opening announcements
- Job posting flyers
- Door drop campaigns (slipped under a door or placed on a windshield)
- Point-of-sale counter displays or bulletin board postings

**Print cost:** 80 lb. gloss text, full color one side, 500 pieces: $95–$130.

### 5.5x8.5 Inch (Half Page)

Cut from the same 11x8.5 sheet as the full-page flyer, but two-up. The half-page format is approximately half the cost to print because you're using the same press sheet. It is not half the impact — for promotions where the message is focused and the contact information is prominent, the half-page format works effectively.

**Best for:** Coupon mailers (the half-page fits naturally in a #10 envelope), take-one stacks on a counter, inserts in a bag or package, simple event announcements.

**Print cost:** Approximately 45–55% of the full-page price for the same quantity, since two pieces print per press sheet.

### 4.25x5.5 Inch (Quarter Page)

Cut four-up from a standard sheet. Very economical at volume. More like a postcard than a traditional flyer — the reduced space requires a focused message with one clear offer or event.

**Best for:** Coupons, appointment reminders, event reminder cards, business-card-style contact pieces.

## Paper Stock for Flyers

The right paper depends on how the flyer will be used:

**Handout flyers, door drops, point-of-sale:** 80 lb. gloss text is the standard. Light enough to be economical, heavy enough to hold up to normal handling, gloss surface makes colors look good. This is what most clients order when cost per piece and speed matter more than premium feel.

**Event flyers for a premium brand or upscale event:** 100 lb. gloss text or 100 lb. matte text. The heavier stock communicates quality — relevant for restaurant openings, gallery events, fundraising galas, or any event where the flyer's quality reflects on the organizer's brand.

**Window decals or outdoor posting:** 100 lb. coated stock with UV coating. The UV finish protects against moisture and handling wear. For bulletin board postings in a weather-exposed location, a thicker stock holds up longer.

**Mailed flyers:** For self-mailing, USPS requires a minimum rigidity standard. An unfolded 8.5x11 on 80 lb. gloss text is borderline — it may pass or may not, depending on the acceptance clerk. For reliable USPS acceptance as a flat, use 80 lb. cover at minimum. For a folded self-mailer, 80 lb. text is adequate.

## Flyers vs. Postcards vs. Brochures: Choosing the Right Format

**Flyer:** Best for broad, low-cost distribution via handout, door drop, or point-of-sale. Not ideal for direct mail (higher postage rate for a flat versus a postcard) or premium sales presentations. Good for events, job postings, seasonal announcements, any campaign where the piece will be distributed by hand rather than mailed.

**Postcard:** Best for direct mail campaigns. The postcard format is designed around USPS postcard and letter rates. More rigid than a flyer, designed to survive USPS handling, better suited for campaigns where postage efficiency matters. For a targeted mail campaign to 2,500 households, order postcards not flyers.

**Brochure:** Best for detailed service explanations, sales support, trade show materials, and take-home reference pieces. Carries significantly more information than a flyer in a compact, professional format. Higher production cost than a flyer but more appropriate for complex service descriptions.

## Design Tips for Effective Flyers

The flyer's job is different from a postcard or brochure — it needs to grab attention from a distance (bulletin board, door, counter) and communicate one idea quickly. These design principles consistently produce better results:

**One dominant visual.** A single large photo or illustration that communicates the subject instantly. A flyer with four small photos reads as cluttered; a flyer with one strong image and a large headline reads from across the room. For event flyers, the photo should show the venue or the experience. For service flyers, before-and-after images work well.

**Headline first, details second.** The headline should take up at least 30% of the flyer's visual space. If the flyer is for an event, the event name and date should be the largest elements. If it's for a service, the offer should dominate. Contact information and supporting details are secondary — they go in the lower third.

**Readable from 3 feet away.** If the flyer will be posted on a bulletin board or window, the headline and key information should be readable from 3 feet. This means minimum 24-point type for headlines and 14-point for supporting text on an 8.5x11 format. A flyer designed for reading distance loses most of its impact when posted.

**Strong call to action.** Every flyer needs to tell the reader what to do: "Call to reserve," "Visit us at [address]," "Use code SPRING25 for 20% off." A flyer without a CTA is a missed opportunity — the reader may appreciate the information but has no prompt to act on it.

## Flyer Distribution Methods in the Hudson Valley

Beyond mailing, flyers have several distribution channels available to Hudson Valley businesses:

**Community bulletin boards.** Most libraries, community centers, laundromats, grocery stores, and coffee shops in towns like New Paltz, Woodstock, Kingston, and Rhinebeck maintain public bulletin boards. Post regulations vary — some require date stamps, some limit flyer size, some require approval from management. A standard 8.5x11 flyer on 80 lb. stock with UV coating holds up best in these environments.

**Door-to-door drops.** Legal in most jurisdictions when placed in a door handle bag or on a doorstep — but not in a USPS mailbox (that is a federal offense reserved for USPS mail). Door drops are labor-intensive but highly targeted — you can walk a specific street or neighborhood and cover exactly the homes you want. For businesses operating in dense downtown areas (Kingston Stockade District, New Paltz Main Street), door drops are an efficient supplement to EDDM.

**Counter displays and take-one racks.** Retail stores, restaurants, and professional offices often allow take-one displays from non-competing businesses. A stack of half-page (5.5x8.5) flyers in a counter display at a complementary business costs nothing to distribute — only the print cost.

**Vehicle windshield drops.** Legal in most parking lots but check local ordinances — some municipalities and private lots prohibit this. Effective for event promotion — a quarter-page (4.25x5.5) flyer under a windshield wiper at a farmers' market, community event, or shopping center parking lot reaches an audience already in your target geographic area.

**Insert in shopping bags.** Local retail stores and restaurants will sometimes insert a flyer from a non-competing business in their shopping bags. This is a form of co-marketing that costs nothing except the flyer itself and reaches a qualified local audience.
